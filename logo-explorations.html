FormGuard · Identity · Session 1 of 2 · Logo

Logo explorations

Coaching, without the coach. A full-body sensor suit that learns how each lifter moves, flags the joint that's off, and tells them how to fix it mid-set. The identity should feel like the tools lifters already trust: a good barbell, a calibrated instrument. Precise, strong, honest, understated.

01

Wordmark typefaces

Six heavy grotesks, condensed to extended, chosen from a long list of 24 (rejected outright: Anybody and Saira for looking like games, Antonio and League Gothic for being generic, Bebas, Oswald and Space Grotesk for being AI and fitness clichés). Every sample is outlined, not live text, and spaced by hand: an optical-gap pass measures the white space between each pair on 120 scanlines, then each pair is judged by eye at 24px and 200px. FORMGUARD in capitals reads as equipment. FormGuard in camel case reads as an app. Recommendation: capitals.

Hubot Sans 800 · width 110

An engineered grotesk with squared curves (super-ellipse O, D, G, U). It reads like the markings on a calibrated instrument: machined, calm, heavy without shouting. Distinctive without being decorative.

Level line · Hubot Sans

F arm, R bowl and G bar share one height

The F's middle arm (421.0) and G's bar (402.5) are moved onto the R's bowl join (406.5), so the word holds one exact level, the way a lifter holds a bar. You don't see it, you feel it. Keep for the final wordmark.

Modified G · Hubot Sans

Hubot's spurred G (left) · spurless G built from its own O (right)

The spurless G is drawn from Hubot's own O bowl, opened at the terminal height (477) and given the same bar, so there's no seam. It's smoother but softer and more generic, like any geometric sans. The square spur under the bar is what makes the word feel machined. Keep the spur. The G's only change is the 4-unit raise onto the level line.

A cut at parallel · Hubot Sans

One horizontal cut through the word at 47% of cap height

The depth line as a cut. It looks like sportswear and like a stencil, and at small sizes it closes up and adds noise. It's an effect, not an idea. Rejected.

Spacing · Pathway Extreme

Font default (top) · hand-kerned, +40 tracking (bottom)

At opsz 72 the defaults are display-tight. They're good for a headline but too tight for a logo that also has to work at 14px. Opened by 4% of cap height, with FO closed −22 and MG opened +8.

Joined pair · Pathway Extreme

U and A locked together

Locking the U into the A's diagonal makes a sports-club ligature. It knots up the middle of GUARD and has nothing to do with form or guarding. Rejected.

02

Mark concepts

Twelve concepts: the seven directions in the brief plus five of my own. Each is exact geometry on a 120-unit grid, shown in one colour on Chalk and on Iron Black. The 32px and 16px tiles are real rasters: each mark is drawn into a 32×32 or 16×16 canvas, so you see the pixels a favicon actually gets (the ×4 tile magnifies the 16px one). Below each: the lockup with the Hubot wordmark, the two-colour version, the stroke weights and gaps measured on the final outline, and the variants I tried and dropped.

Refined: the three

Clean geometry, final weights and kerned lockups. All three share one wordmark: Hubot Sans 760 at width 110, tracked +4.5% of cap height and hand-kerned. The F's middle arm and the G's bar are moved onto the R's bowl join, so the word holds one level, which is the faint red line below. Each mark comes with pixel-drawn 16px and 32px cuts, because a master scaled down to 16px turns to mush. Compare the ×4 tiles.

Kerning on top of the font's own (units/1000 em): FO -28 · OR +11 · RM -6 · MG +11 · GU +6 · UA +14 · AR -23 · RD -6 · level line at 403.87/729 of cap height (F arm, R join, G bar measured identical to 0.01 unit)

Construction

100-unit height. Stem 20; arms 18, lighter by 10% so they look equal to the stem, matching Hubot's own F. Counter 34. The socket (R 22) is tangent to the stem's inner edge and bites 5 into each arm. The bar (r 16) is concentric, so the gap is 6 everywhere. J-hook 84 wide, safety 76 (0.9 of the J-hook, like Hubot's F). Two-colour: only the bar is Plate Red.

Drop-cap lockup: the symbol is the F of FORMGUARD, its stem weight-matched to the wordmark (1.00×) and baseline-aligned. The raised J-hook overhangs the O, and the O sits as far from the safety arm as it does from a regular F. No repeated F.

Recommendation: A, Rack F

It's the only one of the three that couldn't belong to another company. It comes from the name's first letter, says "guard" through a lifter's own equipment rather than a shield, and has real anatomy: rack, joint, bar. The drop-cap lockup makes the symbol the first letter of the wordmark, so mark and word are literally one design. Its pixel cuts hold from a 16px tab to the chest of the suit.

What it asks of us: always use the master artwork (the socket is a precise detail), use the pixel cuts below 48px, and keep red for the bar alone. Parallel is the safer, more abstract instrument. Cradle is the most robust but the least ownable.
